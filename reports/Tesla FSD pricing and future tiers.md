# Tesla FSD subscription pricing is about to get more expensive

Tesla's Full Self-Driving subscription currently costs **$99/month** in the US — a price that has held since 2023 and, as of October 2026, has not yet been raised. But that stability is explicitly temporary. Elon Musk stated on January 23, 2026, that the $99 rate "will rise as FSD's capabilities improve," with the "massive value jump" tied to unsupervised (eyes-off, hands-off) operation. As of February 14, 2026, Tesla eliminated the one-time purchase option entirely, making the monthly subscription the only path to FSD for new buyers. No family plans, annual tiers, or multi-vehicle discounts exist — the only active discount is a 50% reduction to **$49/month** for owners who previously purchased Enhanced Autopilot (EAP). The next major pricing shift is structurally tied to unsupervised FSD reaching consumer vehicles, a milestone that has already slipped past Tesla's end-of-2025 target.

## Tesla moved to subscription-only in February 2026, ending a decade of outright purchases

From Tesla's earliest days selling FSD as a standalone feature, owners could buy it outright — a price that climbed as high as **$15,000 in September 2022**, then dropped to $8,000 by April 2024 as subscription adoption lagged. That ended permanently on **February 14, 2026**, when Tesla stopped offering FSD as a one-time purchase in the US ([PriceTimeline](https://pricetimeline.com/data/price/tesla-fsd-subscription); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/vehicle-tech/dash-cams/tesla-just-switched-to-a-netflix-style-subscription-model-for-full-self-driving-heres-why)). Australia and New Zealand followed on April 1, 2026 ([ZeCar](https://zecar.com/reviews/tesla-pulls-the-plug-on-lifetime-fsd-purchases-in-australia)). A narrow exception exists for buyers of top-tier Model S, Model X, and Cybertruck Cyberbeast through a "Premium Package" bundle that also includes lifetime free Supercharging — but this is irrelevant for Model 3/Y buyers ([News18](https://english.news18a.com/m/news/english_232483.html)).

The shift to subscription-only moved Tesla from a one-time hardware-era revenue model to recurring software revenue. By Q1 2026, Tesla was generating **$546 million in annual recurring FSD subscription revenue** from 476,100 active subscribers ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/4042/tesla-hits-546-million-in-annual-recurring-revenue-from-fsd-subscriptions)), and Q2 2026 subscriber count grew to **1.48 million active users, up 56% year-over-year** ([Not a Tesla App Q2 2026](https://notateslaapp.com/news/4481/summary-of-teslas-2026-q2-earnings-call-cybercab-fsd-ai4-and-more)). This recurring-revenue footing gives Tesla financial incentive to hold the current $99 price long enough to maximize subscriber conversion before the next capability milestone justifies a step-up.

## Hardware splits the subscriber base before pricing even changes

Both HW3 and HW4 (AI4) vehicles are eligible for the same $99/month subscription, but they do not receive the same product. HW4 vehicles currently run FSD v14.3.10 and receive features — including Automatic Collision Evasion — that HW3 vehicles do not. HW3 vehicles run FSD v14.2 Lite, a reduced-capability build ([basenor.com](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/tesla-fsd-in-2026-5-details-that-matter-right-now)). Musk confirmed at Q1 2026 earnings that **HW3 vehicles cannot achieve Unsupervised FSD and cannot join the Robotaxi network** — a permanent ceiling imposed by hardware, not software ([basenor.com](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/tesla-fsd-in-2026-5-details-that-matter-right-now)). Tesla does not offer a retrofit path from HW3 to HW4 due to entirely new wiring and cooling harness requirements ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/2173/how-to-check-if-your-tesla-has-hardware-4-ai4-or-hardware-3)).

This creates a de facto two-tier product at a single price point. A Model Y delivered before roughly late 2023 almost certainly has HW3; one delivered after that cutover has HW4. Both owners pay $99/month today, but the HW3 owner is permanently locked out of the higher-capability tier that will command a higher price. The functional split between HW3 (FSD Lite) and HW4 (full FSD) is already in place — Tesla has the product differentiation ready to monetize with differential pricing, but has not yet done so.

## Musk has explicitly telegraphed a price hike tied to unsupervised FSD

On January 23, 2026, Musk posted on X: **"I should also mention that the $99/month for supervised FSD will rise as FSD's capabilities improve."** In the same post: "The massive value jump is when you can be on your phone or sleeping for the entire ride (unsupervised FSD)." ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3534/musk-says-fsd-subscription-prices-will-rise-as-capabilities-increase); [Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/elon-musk-warns-of-fsd-subscription-price-increases/)). These statements were made the same day Tesla announced it was ending the one-time purchase option and discontinuing Autopilot in North America — a deliberate pricing strategy announcement, not an offhand comment.

The Q1 2025 earnings call had already set this direction. Tesla stated FSD is **"too cheap at $99/mo as it currently stands"** and that the company "will consider pricing options for Unsupervised FSD vs Supervised FSD" ([Not a Tesla App Q1 2025 earnings](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/2666/tesla-shares-new-details-on-fsd-unsupervised-robotaxi-potential-fsd-price-hike-during-q1-2025-earnings-call)). Neither the Q1 nor Q2 2026 earnings calls contained new pricing statements — confirming that the increase is tied to the unsupervised milestone, not being implemented incrementally.

No official dollar figure for unsupervised FSD has been named. The **"$300+/month"** figures circulating in media are community speculation extrapolated from Musk's "massive value jump" framing, not any Tesla announcement. Industry analysts have speculated a supervised tier of $150–$199/month and an unsupervised tier "significantly higher," but these are unconfirmed ([tesery.com](https://www.tesery.com/blogs/news/tesla-confirms-upcoming-price-hike-for-full-self-driving-subscriptions-as-capabilities-evolve)).

The timing risk is real: Musk had committed to unsupervised FSD on consumer vehicles by end of 2025, missed that deadline, and as of Q1 2026 earnings pushed the target to Q4 2026 ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/04/22/tesla-elon-musk-unsupervised-fsd-consumer-cars-q4-delay-again/)). Robotaxi without safety drivers is operational in Austin, Dallas, and Houston as of Q2 2026, which is a material step — but consumer vehicle unsupervised FSD is a separate regulatory and deployment challenge, and the Q4 2026 target had not been reaffirmed at Q2 2026 earnings. The direction is credible; the timeline is not.

## No family plans, annual tiers, or multi-vehicle discounts exist

Tesla's FSD subscription is strictly per-vehicle and month-to-month. There is no annual plan, no household bundle, no fleet pricing, and no cancellation fee — access simply continues through the end of the paid billing period ([CarsDirect](https://www.carsdirect.com/automotive-news/tesla-fsd-is-going-subscription-only); [lowermysubs.com](https://www.lowermysubs.com/blog/tesla-fsd-worth-it-2026)).

The only active discount is the **$49/month rate for prior Enhanced Autopilot purchasers**, effective January 29, 2026, available in both the US and Canada ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3539/tesla-reduces-fsd-subscription-to-49month-for-vehicles-with-enhanced-autopilot)). This is framed as compensation for owners who paid for a feature Tesla later discontinued — not a general loyalty program. Current $99/month subscribers have no documented price protection if rates increase.

One signal worth watching: a Tesla app UI was observed asking users to "verify a vehicle match," which sparked speculation that multi-vehicle subscriptions might be under development ([basenor.com](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/could-tesla-fsd-subscription-soon-work-across-multiple-cars)). This is architectural groundwork for per-vehicle subscription tracking, not confirmation of a family plan. Tesla Insurance does offer a multi-car discount for multiple vehicles on the same policy ([Tesla Insurance Support](https://www.tesla.com/support/insurance/additional-coverages-discounts)), but that is entirely separate from FSD software pricing and provides no indication of software discount plans.

## Conclusion

Tesla's FSD pricing is stable today at $99/month but deliberately positioned as a floor, not a ceiling. The company has publicly committed to raising it when unsupervised autonomy reaches consumer vehicles — the only question is when, and by how much. For a new Model Y buyer, the most consequential variable is hardware: an HW4 vehicle remains eligible for whatever the future unsupervised tier looks like; an HW3 vehicle is permanently capped at supervised FSD regardless of subscription price. The absence of any annual, family, or fleet discount option means there is no structural way to reduce the per-vehicle monthly cost beyond qualifying for the EAP discount — and no indication Tesla plans to add one.
